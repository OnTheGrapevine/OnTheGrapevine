ITEM: sewing machine
BRAND: Elna
MODEL: Lotus tsp
YEAR OF PRODUCTION: model series from 1975

PROBLEM: motor not engaging with the mechanical system driving the needle

REPAIR:

  1. did a visual PAT inspection. Plug needs rewiring as some coppoer was exposed. I still continued tests at my own discretion, everything else seemed fine.
  2. mechanical system was working perfectly when turning the hand wheel. when plugged in, both the light and bobbin winder mode were working fine, but when in sewing mode, i could hear the motor running but the mechanical system wasn't engaging.
  3. i opened up the top panel and bottom panel but struggled to pry open the side panel, where the issue was, so i could not diagnose the problem. called it a day.
  4. brought the sewing machine to The Wheel Merton Repair Cafe, where i got it PAT tested (passed) and Martin advised i change the 13A fuse to 3A and rewire the plug.
  5. on a second day attempt at home i was finally able to pry open the side panel. luckily i didn't break anything, because i realised only after opening, there was an easier way to do it
  6. at first i was baffled at where the motor would even connect to the mechanical system. after asking the Signal GC for Tooting Repair Club, i researched about how know clutch mechanisms work to engage the needle.
  7. i disassembled the motor from the side panel to inspect the clutch and see how it works. after looking and researching to understand how the clutch worked, there seemed to be no issues, so i decided to reassemble the machine and test it again because sometimes machines work after a 'magic touch'. when reassembling i made sure to screw the spring so it was a tight as it could be, as that may have been the issue
  8. low and behold, the magic touch worked and the motor was now engaging with the wheel! however, the wheel was not properly connected to the mechanical system driving the needle. so i had to dissaemble and reassemble the side panel one more time to make sure everything was connected properly
  9. all it needs now is cleaning and servicing

OUTCOME: very happy success!! -as this is my first complety unsupervised repair and my own sewing machine so i can start textile repair at home! :)

ITEM: sewing machine
BRAND: unkown
MODEL: unkown

REPAIR:
  1. shadowing Martin for this repair
  2. PAT test: pass
  3. pulling the thread on the needle straight back was causing the needle to bend more than expected. this is a tension test as the thread is supposed to feed with an even tension and the needle is not supposed to bend that much. the bending means that the tension was catching somewhere
  4. we opened up the machine to check the tension discs. after flossing some dust out, the tension seemed to be fine, so it must have been catching somewhere else
  5. Martin spent a while adjusting the parts and pulling thread through the machine to see where the tension was catching but couldn't figure it out
  6. unfortunately during this time, a small spacer piece fell onto the floor and we wasn't able to find it until the end of the repair cafe

  2. Jonathan had concerns with the plug being rated 7.5V but outputting 12+V. apparently radio circuitboards can just die if a higher voltage plug is used.

  4. we opened up the radio just incase we missed something and when testing it plugged in whilst dissassembled, i noticed a tiny spark on the main circuitboard. Jonathan realised this was because a soldered conection had been disconnected
  5. the connection was resoldered and the radio resassembled
OUTCOME: success! radio now turning on normally and not intermittently
